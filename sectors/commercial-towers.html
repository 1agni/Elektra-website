Sectors · 01

Commercial towers

The city’s default building. A plant in the basement or on the roof, floors that lease and empty, a law with a carbon number.

Who it is for

Owners, asset managers, and chief engineers of Class A and solid Class B towers. Hudson Yards, Midtown, FiDi, Downtown Brooklyn. Buildings whose identity is the lobby and whose risk is the plant.

Typical systems

Central chilled water, cooling towers, VAV with reheat, steam or hydronic heat, increasingly heat recovery and heat pumps. BAS that ranges from Niagara to something last touched in 2004. Garage exhaust, lobby DOAS, a trading floor that thinks it is a data hall.

Constraints

After-hours occupancy that the original load never contemplated. Stack effect. Neighbors who hear towers. Con Edison lead times. LL97 2030. A board that wants a number, not a seminar. Elevators that are the only path for a chiller barrel.

Our point of view

We do not romanticize the office tower. We make its climate invisible and its pathway legal. Right-size before you replace. Sequence before you add tons. Treat the roof as architecture. The related project is One Manhattan West — a plant that got smaller, quieter, and cheaper to run.
