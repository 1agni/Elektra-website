Refrigeration

Cold and hot, on the same roof.

Walk-ins, reach-ins, racks, ice, prep tables. Then the ranges, ovens, steamers, and hoods that reject heat onto the same real estate. One practice, because the physics already was.

Why HVAC and refrigeration belong together

A restaurant does not have a climate problem and a product problem. It has a roof, a service, a grease duct, a walk-in that dumps heat into a kitchen that is already too hot, and a condenser that the landlord’s engineer never saw on the original drawing. Split those trades and you get a hood that starves, a box that ices, and a terrace neighbor with a lawyer. We keep them in one company so heat rejection, electrical, and roof choreography are designed once. We wrote an essay about that.

Grocery, commissary, catering, hotel kitchens, institutional food service: same argument, larger racks. The commercial HVAC practice is next door when the dining room or the hotel tower shares a plant. The account team can hold both.

What we tend

Walk-in coolers and freezers. Reach-ins. Parallel racks. Ice machines that are not an afterthought. Prep tables and undercounter. Remote condensers and the structural dunnage they sit on. Leak search that is a method, not a sniff in passing. Then the hot side: hoods, makeup, cooking equipment as a sibling page, not a subcontractor we met at the dock.
