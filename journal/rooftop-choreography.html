Rooftop choreography

Towers, screens, structure, neighbors, the skyline. A roof is the last elevation of the building. Treat it as leftover and the city will notice.

New York roofs are crowded. Cooling towers, condensers, grease fans, generators, dunnage on dunnage, a screen that was a condition of the last variance, a terrace the amenity deck people won in 2019. Into this we are asked to put a quieter, heavier, more efficient plant, on a Sunday, without waking the building to the west. This is choreography. It is not a catalog pick.

The roof is the one elevation everyone above you can see and everyone beside you can hear. Architects know this and then, under budget, forget it. Mechanical contractors know the crane and then, under schedule, forget the neighbor. Our job is to remember both at the same time.

Structure is the first drawing

A cooling tower is a wet, vibrating load. The steel under it is often 1980s, already carrying more than the original set imagined, with a dunnage that was a “temporary” from a previous replacement. We do not set a machine on a story. We ask for a structural engineer, cores if we need them, and a dunnage that is a drawing. If the roof cannot take the tower you want, you do not get that tower. You get a different machine, a different location, or a different conversation with the owner. Gravity is not a stakeholder you can workshop.

Crane days are the public performance of this fact. Weight, radius, wind, DOT, a street that will not close, a Sunday that will. Two mornings at EDITION; a March weekend at Manhattan West. The choreography includes the police, the hotel guests, the wind forecast, and a plan B that is not “we’ll try in June.”

Noise is a property line

The code has a number. The neighbor has a lawyer. We design to the neighbor. Low-noise fans, VFDs that actually turn down at night, screens that are absorptive where they need to be and open where the tower needs to breathe. A sound model to the nearest residential window, not to a point in space that makes us look good. Five dBA under the limit is a courtesy that prevents a year of letters.

Kitchen exhaust is a special case of the same problem: odor, plume, grease, FDNY, and a fan that will scream if you let it. High-plume dilution, a pollution-control unit when the program needs it, makeup air so the building does not steal from itself. The roof is where hospitality either grows up or does not.

The screen is architecture

A louvered box is not a screen. A screen has a proportion, a material, a relationship to the cornice, a maintenance door that is not a wound. We work with the architect because the alternative is a plant that photographs as a failure. LPC, if you are in their jurisdiction, would like a mockup. Give them one. Renderings of rooftop equipment are lies at a particular scale; a sample of the metal is not.

Maintenance access is part of the elevation. If a technician cannot get to a basin without a harness and a prayer, the basin will not be maintained, and the quiet tower will become a loud one with a biological problem. We draw the walkway. We specify the rail. We are boring on purpose.

Neighbors, hours, water

Cooling towers blow down. Drift. Legionella programs. Basin heaters. The building next door has outdoor air intakes you should have noticed. We notice. After-hours work is not only for the occupants below; it is for the block. A crane at 4am is a negotiation. A tower start-up at 4am is a mistake.

Water on a roof is a leak story waiting for a lawyer. Containment, leak detection where the program is unkind (data, hospitals), isolation valves that a human can reach. The choreography includes the day the basin overflows because someone left a valve in the wrong place. Design that day.

The skyline is a client

We are a mechanical practice in a city that is looked at. The invisible system is allowed to be visible on the roof if it is composed. If it is a forest, we failed. Rooftop choreography is the unromantic name for making sure the last elevation is still a building. Towers, screens, structure, neighbors, noise. Then the plant, which was the point all along, running without commentary.
