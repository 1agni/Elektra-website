One practice

HVAC, refrigeration, and kitchen are not three vendors. In New York they share a roof, a service, and a night.

A restaurant group called us about a walk-in. That is the polite version. The true version is that the dining room was cold, the pass was impossible, the condenser on the roof was cycling against a kitchen fan that had been aimed at it, and the landlord’s engineer had three different companies leaving three different closeouts in the same folder. Nobody was wrong in their own scope. The building was wrong as a system.

We are a mechanical practice. For a decade that meant commercial HVAC: plants, sequences, the quiet that follows. Residences arrived because the same physics showed up in penthouses, with millwork instead of a VAV box. Refrigeration and kitchen arrived because the same roof showed up in food. This is not a growth story. It is an admission that New York does not separate heat the way org charts do.

Heat has to go somewhere

A walk-in rejects heat. A hood exhausts it, and a makeup unit puts some of it back, conditioned or not. A dining room HVAC system tries to hold 72°F against a pass that is doing something else. If those machines are sized by three vendors who have not seen each other’s drawings, the roof becomes an argument and the electrical service becomes a surprise. We have stood on that roof. It is always smaller than the last meeting implied.

Heat reclaim is the unglamorous correction. Warewash wants hot water. Racks reject heat all afternoon. Connecting them is arithmetic. It only happens when the people who own the racks also own the water heater conversation. Split the trades and the reclaim coil is a line item someone will value-engineer into a regret.

The roof is the last elevation

We already wrote about rooftop choreography as architecture. Food makes it worse. Condensers, exhaust fans, makeup intakes, grease, a screen the neighbor can see, a structure that was designed for a tar roof and a water tank. Landmark bulkheads. A terrace that is someone’s penthouse. If HVAC and refrigeration arrive as two crane days, you get two screens and a fight. If they arrive as one set, you get a frame, a sequence, and a property-line number you can defend.

Electrical is a single story

Service upgrades in New York are measured in years. A kitchen that adds a rack, a hood, and a rooftop unit as three peaks will find the panel first and the opening date second. One practice can write one peak, stagger starts, and tell the truth about a feeder. Three practices can each be “within the load we were given.”

Night is a single desk

A chiller down, a penthouse with no cooling, a walk-in leaving its band: the person who answers should not have to decide which company you meant. Product loss is a clock. A residential no-cool is a household. A plant emergency is a floor of tenants. We staff one desk because the city does not pause to read a scope exhibit. Agreement sites go first. Everyone else hears an honest ETA.

Residences are not a dilution

A luxury apartment is not a small office. Finishes, freight, the super, quiet hours, a board packet — these are constraints as hard as a hospital’s infection control, just quieter. We took residences because the equipment became serious: VRF heat recovery, hydronic in masonry, wine rooms, DOAS in tight envelopes. We did not take them to fill a calendar with filter changes. The suburban split-system remains a no. The full-floor apartment that must stay livable is a yes. The threshold is the architecture and the equipment, both.

What we will not pretend

We are not a restaurant-supply catalog. We are not a coupon residential brand. We are not three companies in a trench coat. We are eighty-five people who can put a PE, a superintendent, a refrigeration technician, and a residential tech on the same account when the owner holds a tower, a penthouse, and a commissary. Sometimes that owner is three different people. The physics does not care.

If your job is only a chiller, call the commercial practice. If it is only a walk-in, call refrigeration. If it is a kitchen on a roof that already has HVAC, call us once. We will tell you if we are the right studio. We will not invent a fourth vendor so that nobody has to be responsible for the intake that faces the exhaust.

Hudson Square for the paper. LIC for the truck. The desk at night. One name on the closeout.
