03 · Midtown · Hospitality · 2021

EDITION Times Square

Location

701 Seventh Avenue, Times Square

Year

2021

Client

Hospitality owner / operator

Scope

Quiet guest-room HVAC, kitchen exhaust, and a rooftop that does not fight the skyline or the neighbors.

Systems

Guest-room VRF with dedicated outdoor air at the corridor. Kitchen grease exhaust to a rooftop pollution-control unit, then a high-plume fan. Makeup air tempered and interlocked. Rooftop air-cooled heat pumps behind a custom screen. Sound attenuators on every outdoor unit cluster. Guest-room NC-25 target. Central BAS with room-level occupancy from the hotel lock system.

The problem

Times Square is a noise ordinance with a marketing department. The previous rooftop was a forest of condensing units, a grease fan that could be heard on 47th, and guest-room PTAC units that announced themselves every time they cycled. Reviews mentioned the HVAC. That is a failure of hospitality, not a mechanical footnote.

The kitchen is a revenue floor. Exhaust cannot be “value-engineered” into a complaint. Landmark-adjacent views meant the screen wall had to be an architectural object. Structural reserve on the roof was thin — 1980s steel, already carrying more than it was drawn for.

The approach

We took the PTACs out. VRF evaporators in bulkheads, line sets in existing chases, DOAS at 0.3 CFM/sf to the corridor with transfer to rooms under the door — the quiet way to do outdoor air in a hotel. Occupancy signals from the lock vendor: a vacant room goes to setback without a guest ever seeing a thermostat war.

Kitchen: new welded steel riser, pollution-control unit, 12,000 CFM, high-plume dilution. Makeup air at 80% of exhaust, heated, interlocked, commissioned with the chef present. Rooftop units clustered, isolated, screened. We ran a sound model to the nearest residential window on 47th and designed to 5 dBA under code, not to code.

Crane: two Sunday mornings, NYPD assist, 4am–10am. After that, everything went up the service elevator in pieces. FDNY inspected the grease riser. DOB filed the alteration. The architect signed the screen.

The outcome

Guest-room measurements: NC-23 to NC-26 with the VRF at high. Review language about HVAC disappeared in two quarters. Kitchen stack odor complaints from the block: zero in the first year. The roof reads as a piece of the building, not a plant. Energy for guest-room conditioning down 34% versus the PTAC baseline.
