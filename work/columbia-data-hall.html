05 · Manhattanville · Research computing · 2024

Columbia Data Hall

Location

Manhattanville campus, West Harlem

Year

2024

Client

University research computing

Scope

High-density cooling for a 2.5 MW IT hall. Rear-door heat exchangers, in-row support, leak detection, N+1 that means N+1.

Systems

2.5 MW IT load. Rear-door heat exchangers on the GPU rows (30–40 kW/rack). In-row CRAH units on the CPU rows. Chilled water from a dedicated 1,000-ton N+1 plant — two 500-ton screw machines, one spare. 46°F CHW to the rear doors, 55°F to the in-row. Spot leak detection under every cold aisle and at every CHW valve. Dual-corded CRAH. Aspirating smoke. No water over the aisle unless it is in a pipe we can isolate in seconds.

The problem

Research computing does not care about your weekend. A training run that dies at 3am is a grant conversation on Monday. The previous hall was air-cooled at 8 kW/rack and had run out of both tons and floor. The new hall asked for 35 kW on the GPU row. That is not a CRAH conversation. That is a water conversation, done carefully, over a live campus.

The building is not a purpose-built data center. Floor-to-floor is academic. Structural capacity was a study, not an assumption. The university’s sustainability office asked for a cooling pPUE, not a shrug.

The approach

We put water in the door, not in the aisle. Rear-door HX for the dense row, in-row refrigerated air for the rest. A dedicated plant so a campus chiller trip does not take research with it. N+1 is two machines able to carry the hall, plus a valved stub for a rental if both are down — which they should not be.

Leak detection: addressable cables, mapped in the BAS, with automatic isolation valves on every row manifold. We commissioned the fail-close. Twice. Sequences: CHW reset against rear-door return, not against an empty setpoint. Containment on the cold aisle. Electrical coordination with the UPS vendor so CRAH dual-cords mean something.

Work in an occupied academic building: after 7pm, loading dock on 125th, quiet during lectures. DOB, campus EH&S, and the research computing group in the same weekly meeting. No surprises on a blackboard.

The outcome

Hall accepted at 2.4 MW IT with headroom to 2.5. Cooling pPUE 1.15 at typical load. Leak-detection false positives in month one: three; after sensitivity tuning, zero. A campus chilled-water outage in August did not touch the hall. The GPU row sits at 37 kW/rack without throwing air at the ceiling.
