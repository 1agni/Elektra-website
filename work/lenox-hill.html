02 · Upper East Side · Healthcare · 2022

Lenox Hill

Location

100 East 77th Street, Upper East Side

Year

2022

Client

Hospital campus / acute care wing

Scope

Redundant cooling for an occupied wing. Acoustic isolation. 24/7 construction that cannot take the floor down.

Systems

N+1 chilled water: 2 × 400-ton magnetic-bearing centrifugals, one always in reserve. Dual-path air handlers serving ORs and the ICU with HEPA terminal filtration. Dedicated outdoor air with enthalpy wheels. Isolation rooms at negative pressure with monitored offsets. Spring-isolated plant on a mid-rise mechanical floor. BAS with critical-space alarming to engineering and to nursing.

The problem

The wing was staying open. That sentence governed every other sentence. The existing 1980s plant had a single 600-ton machine and a standby that had not been run in earnest in years. A bearing failure in July would have been a diversion event. Infection control needed OR pressure relationships held during construction. The floor below the mechanical room is a sleep-study unit. Vibration was a clinical issue, not a comfort one.

Utility shutdowns on this block are political. Con Edison windows are overnight, rare, and revoked. We had to assume the existing electrical service and make N+1 fit inside it.

The approach

We built a temporary 250-ton rental skid on the loading dock roof, piped into the secondary loop, commissioned it, and only then released the old machine. Magnetic-bearing compressors, because oil management in a hospital plant is a failure mode we would rather not own. Isolation inertia bases, 2-inch deflection springs, and a floating floor in the mechanical room. Duct silencers on every discharge that faces a patient floor.

OR air is dual-path: a clean recirculation unit with HEPA, and a DOAS with enthalpy recovery that never mixes with isolation exhaust. Pressure relationships were mapped room-by-room with facilities and infection control before a single tap. Work hours for noisy trades: 7pm–5am, with a quiet-hours protocol posted at the nursing station. FDNY reviewed the temporary fuel for the rental skid. DOB and DOHMH paperwork ran in parallel, not in series.

The outcome

Cooling availability during construction: 100%. Post-occupancy, the plant has not had an unplanned dual-machine outage. OR pressure alarms dropped by an order of magnitude after the dual-path conversion. Sleep-study staff reported the mechanical floor as “finally gone.” The hospital kept the rental skid for two extra weeks as a courtesy; they did not need it.
