06 · Grand Army Plaza · Civic · 2023

Brooklyn Public Library

Location

10 Grand Army Plaza, Brooklyn

Year

2023

Client

Civic / public library (Central)

Scope

Occupancy-based ventilation in a landmarked reading room. Historic constraints. A plant that does not rewrite the architecture.

Systems

Dedicated outdoor air with enthalpy wheels serving the catalog rooms and the great hall. Existing steam-to-hot-water conversion for perimeter radiation, kept as architecture. CO₂ and occupancy counting for ventilation reset. MERV-13, with a path to MERV-14. Discreet floor and bulkhead devices — no exposed duct in the hall. BAS trending to facilities, not to a vendor cloud the City does not own.

The problem

The great hall is a civic room. You do not hang duct in it. You do not replace windows that the Landmarks Preservation Commission has already spent a decade arguing about. You do not heat it like an office. The existing system was a patchwork of steam, leftover dual-duct, and good intentions. In summer the west windows cooked the tables. In winter the perimeter hissed. Ventilation was a constant 1990s number, whether the room held forty people or four hundred.

Budget was public. Shutdowns were political. The building stays open. The work had to read as care, not as a renovation that won.

The approach

We hid the air. DOAS on the attic structure, distribution in existing chases, supply at the perimeter through rebuilt cabinets that match the 1941 millwork. Enthalpy wheels because Brooklyn summer air is a load. Occupancy cameras that count bodies, not faces, and CO₂ as the backup. When the hall is empty, we still keep the books in range; we do not overventilate an absence.

Steam remains at the boiler house; we converted to hot water at the wing so the radiators stay. LPC reviewed every grille. We submitted mockups, not renderings. After-hours construction, union labor, a public-facing weekly notice in the lobby. Commissioning included a Saturday with the librarians, because they know the room.

The outcome

Ventilation energy down 32% with occupancy reset. Summer dry-bulb at the west tables within 2°F of the east. Steam leaks in the wing: gone. LPC approved the visible work without a second hearing. The hall still sounds like a hall — pages, not air. The City owns the trends.
