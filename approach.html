Approach

Listen. Measure. Draw. Build. Tend.

Five chapters. A job is staffed, commissioned, handed over, and then — if you want us — kept. The same sequence in a plant, an apartment, and a kitchen; the manners change.

01

Listen

The building, the operator, the household, the chef, the law, the neighbor. Constraints before options.

02

Measure

Nameplates, trends, a week of tons, a wine-room logger. We do not size from a memory.

03

Draw

Basis of design, PE set when required, sequence in English. The paper the field will use.

04

Build

Coordinated installation. Shutdowns, freight, floor protection. Start-up with a name on it.

05

Tend

Commissioning, handover, the agreement. Account teams, parts in LIC, a desk at night.

Listen

A first conversation is not a pitch. We want the address, the practice (commercial, residences, refrigeration, kitchen), the constraint you are not putting in the RFP — the neighbor, the board, the steam date, the OR schedule, the tasting menu, the quiet hours. If we are not the right studio, we say so. A suburban split-system is a no. A job we cannot staff is a no. Flattery is a no.

Measure

Walk the plant, the closet, the box. Read the tags. Pull a month of intervals if they exist. A temporary meter if they do not. Acoustic spot checks when the roof or the bedroom wall is the question. Occupancy that matches badge data, or a household that actually cooks. Measurement is how we avoid selling you a 1,200-ton machine for an 800-ton building, or a VRF indoor unit that cannot fit the millwork.

Draw

The basis of design is the contract in prose. Loads, outdoor air, acoustics, redundancy, carbon, phasing, floor protection. Then the set: PE-stamped when required, coordinated with structure, architecture, millwork, or the hood. Sequences written before they are programmed. We file. We answer the examiner. We do not outsource the thinking and stamp the residue.

Build

A superintendent who has done this borough — or a residential lead who has done this management office. Union labor as the site requires. Crane days, COIs, loading docks, freight cabs, after-hours. Temporary services so the occupied floor, apartment, or line does not learn about our work the hard way. Start-up is a procedure. Vendors attend; they do not own the day.

Tend

Commissioning is not a certificate. It is trends that match the sequence, a punch list that closes, and a training that the night engineer — or the house manager — can repeat. Then the optional remaining: a commercial agreement (preventive, full labor, full coverage), a residential agreement with named techs, refrigeration after close. Parts in LIC. The emergency desk. A capital conversation every autumn.

How a job is staffed

Every project has a principal (Maya Chen, PE, or Luis Navarro), a project engineer, a superintendent or residential/refrigeration lead once we are in the field, and a BAS lead if sequences are in scope. Healthcare adds an infection-control liaison. Food adds Rafael Mendoza’s bench. Homes add Elena Lowe’s. Account teams sit across practices when the owner holds more than one kind of building. The emergency desk is the overlay that keeps 24/7 from being a myth. Eighty-five people is enough to put names on a chart and mean them.
