Maintenance

The unglamorous half of climate. Filters, coils, belts, bearings, chemicals, seasons, logbooks. A relationship, not a ticket queue.

What it is

Maintenance is how a plant keeps the promise the drawing made. We write a program from the equipment list, not from a generic binder. Seasonal changeover that happens before the first hot Tuesday. Coil cleaning that is photographed. Filters that are MERV as specified, not as substituted. Vibration readings that have a history. Water treatment with tests, not drums in a corner.

We keep logbooks the DOB and the insurance engineer can read. We trend the BAS so a technician arrives already knowing the story. The SLA names response times, after-hours coverage, and who is on the rotating weekend. Eighty-five people is enough to mean it.

When a building needs it

When the last contractor was a van and a promise. When the chief engineer is one retirement away from tribal knowledge. When LL97 made “we’ll see” an expensive sentence. When a hospital, a hotel, or a data hall cannot discover a failed belt on Monday morning.

How we work

A site walk. An asset list. A gap analysis against manufacturer and against the law. Then a program: weekly, monthly, seasonal, annual. Named technicians, not a rotating stranger. Parts that matter are stocked in LIC, not hoped for. We will tell you when a machine is done — before it tells you.

Reporting is a monthly letter the owner can forward, and a portal the engineer can live in. We do not bury findings in a PDF no one opens. If a cooling tower needs a basin this winter, it is in October’s note.

What you get

  • Preventive calendar tied to assets
  • Seasonal changeover and filter/coil discipline
  • Vibration, oil, refrigerant, and water logs
  • Written SLA with 24/7 escalation into emergency
  • Quarterly on-site review with engineering
  • A capital list that is honest about remaining life

On site

Visit-by-visit

A plant visit and a floor walk are different days. Mixing them is how the chiller log stays empty and the VAV list stays fiction.

Plant visit

  • Sign in. LOTO as required before any open work.
  • Chiller: oil, approach, ΔT, starter/VFD, run hours vs. last log.
  • Tower: basin, fill, drift, strainers, fan/gearbox, makeup.
  • Pumps: alignment, seals, strainers, isolation that still isolates.
  • AHU serving critical air: filters (MERV as specified), belts, coil face, drain, damper stroke.
  • Boiler in season: combustion, safeties, condensate.
  • BAS: failed points, overrides left on, sequence vs. the written SOO.
  • Water treatment: test with the chemist, or name the gap.
  • Photograph coils and open conditions. Write the log the same day.
  • Verbal to the chief before leaving the dock. Parts on order named with a date.

Floor walk

  • Zone list from the last visit, not a new wander.
  • VAV / fan-coil: damper action, reheat, discharge sensor, filter where fitted.
  • Hot/cold complaints mapped to boxes, not to “the system.”
  • Condensate, drip, and ceiling stains — photograph, do not guess.
  • Occupied-floor manners: finishes, quiet hours, freight only.
  • Seasonal only unless the SLA says quarterly on that floor.
  • Findings into the capital letter and the next plant visit, not a chat with the porter.
  • Boxes that have been overridden since July are named, with who overrode them if the BAS will say.

Questions

Is this a call-when-it-breaks contract?

No. That is emergency. Maintenance is scheduled work plus the right to escalate.

Can you maintain a plant you did not install?

Yes. We start with a condition survey so we are not inheriting fiction.

What is the SLA?

Named response times, after-hours coverage, parts stocking, and a monthly report. Written. Specific to the site.
