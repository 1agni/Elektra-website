Duct cleaning

Sometimes the air path is the problem. Sometimes it is a brochure. We will tell you which, before anyone brings a vacuum into a finished ceiling.

When it is worth it

Construction dust left in a new or renovated system. A grease-laden kitchen exhaust that has skipped its cycle. Biological growth after a coil that ran wet for a season. A healthcare or lab duct that failed a particle or hygiene look. Fire or water events. These are reasons. “It has never been cleaned” is a sentence, not a diagnosis.

We open a representative run, photograph, and — if the BAS or the IAQ numbers argue for it — scope further. NADCA-style methods where they apply. We do not sell a building-wide clean because a single diffuser was grey.

When it is not

A well-filtered commercial office with intact lining and no event is rarely transformed by duct cleaning. The coil, the filter rack, and the outdoor-air intake are usually the better money. We will say so. A residential “air duct special” is not a service we offer; if a penthouse has a real contamination event, that is a scoped job with floor protection, not a coupon.

NYC ducts, lining, finishes

Flexible runouts, internal lining, and historic plaster do not forgive a careless brush. Occupied floors mean negative machines that actually negative, containment at the grille, and a schedule that the tenant already agreed to. We do not tear lining to chase a photograph. We do not wet a ceiling that cannot take it. Access panels that we cut get finished as specified, not taped and forgotten.

Kitchen hoods and grease ducts are a different discipline — frequency, wrapping, FDNY. That work lives with the kitchen practice when it is a restaurant; commercial office cafés still come through this page with the same standard.

How we work

Walk, photograph, sample if needed. A written scope: which risers, which floors, which method, which protection. After-hours in occupied towers. HEPA, agitation only where the lining allows, verification photographs, a closeout the engineer can keep. If the real problem was the coil, we clean the coil and stop.

What you get

  • A recommendation to clean — or not — with reasons
  • Protection of finishes, lining, and occupied floors
  • Documented before and after, not a van decal
  • Coordination with IAQ and controls when the air was the complaint

Indoor air quality is often the better first conversation.

Questions

Do you always recommend cleaning?

No. We recommend it when the duct is the problem. Coils and filtration are more often the problem.

Will you clean a lined system?

Carefully, or not at all. Destroying lining to ‘clean’ it is how you buy a replacement duct.

Residential ducts?

Only as a scoped job in the residences practice, with the same finish discipline. Not a package rate.
