Indoor air quality

Air as a specified material. Filtration, pressurization, outdoor air, healthcare and lab. Measured after occupancy, not promised in a brochure.

What it is

Indoor air quality is ventilation, filtration, pressurization, and humidity — held, not hoped. We design outdoor air to the occupancy you have, with demand control where the room empties. We specify MERV (and HEPA where the program requires it) as a system, including pressure drop the fans can actually carry. We hold pressure relationships in healthcare, labs, and any room that has a reason to push or pull.

Post-occupancy we measure: particles, CO₂ as a ventilation proxy, pressure offsets, humidity. The report is for the owner and for the people who filed the complaint. We do not sell a canister of something and call it a strategy.

When a building needs it

A hospital, always. A school or a civic reading room with variable occupancy. A lab. A tower whose tenants are asking questions the last fit-out cannot answer. After a construction event. After a wildfire week, if you want the filter story to be true.

How we work

Program first: who is in the room, what the room is for, what the code and the guideline say (ASHRAE 62.1, 170 for healthcare, the owner’s own standard if it is stricter). Then the system: DOAS, dedicated exhaust, filtration stages, monitoring. Then the verification. IAQ is not a product. It is a performance.

We will push back on “maximum outdoor air” as a personality. Energy, humidity, and the plant have opinions. The right outdoor air is the outdoor air the room needs, recovered when we can, filtered always.

What you get

  • Ventilation and filtration design to a named standard
  • Pressurization maps for healthcare and lab
  • Monitoring (CO₂, particles, offset) where it is useful
  • Post-occupancy measurement and a written report
  • Filter standards and change calendars that match the design

New York

Healthcare: DOHMH, FGI, ASHRAE 170, infection control as a signature on the drawing. Labs: directional airflow that EH&S will walk. Civic: LPC if the grille is visible. Wildfire and summer ozone are not California stories; they arrive here. We design filtration as if they will.

After occupancy

A design that is never measured is a hope. We return with a particle counter, a pressure map, and a CO₂ log in the rooms that matter. If a tenant complaint started the work, the report is written so that complaint can end. Filter change calendars are part of handover, not an afterthought in a maintenance year. If the fans cannot carry MERV-14, we say so in the BOD — we do not specify a filter the plant will bypass.

Questions

We specify proven filtration and ventilation. We do not sell devices that outrun their evidence.

Can you test an existing building?

Yes. A measurement study is often the first honest page.

Healthcare only?

No. Healthcare is the strictest. Offices, civic, and hospitality ask, and we answer in their language.
