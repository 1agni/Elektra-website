Service agreements

Three shapes. Not a PDF with a gold stamp. A building, an asset list, a named team, and a tradeoff you can explain to a board.

The tradeoff

Time, quality, and cost do not all sit at the maximum. A cheaper agreement visits less often, stocks less, and still calls the same emergency desk — it just waits in a different line. A full-coverage agreement costs more in January so that August is a conversation, not a capital surprise. We will not sell you the expensive one because it is the expensive one. We will tell you which failure modes you are actually buying out.

All three include a dedicated account team: a project engineer who knows the plant, technicians who return, a dispatcher who already has the freight instructions. Parts that fail on a schedule live in LIC. After-hours is written, not implied. Union sites stay union sites.

01 — Preventive

Scheduled care. Repairs extra.

Inspections, filters, coils, belts, chemicals, seasonal changeover, a logbook, a monthly note. Labor to repair is time-and-material at contracted rates. Priority at the emergency desk over a building we have never seen. This is the honest minimum for a plant that still has a competent in-house engineer.

02 — Full labor

Preventive, plus the hours when it breaks.

Everything in Preventive, and the labor to diagnose and repair during the term. Parts remain extra — named, not padded. This is the usual shape for a Class A tower or a hotel that does not want to argue about hours at 2am. Capital replacements (a finished compressor, a condemned tower) are still capital. We will flag them in autumn, not in a heat wave.

03 — Full coverage

Labor and the wearing parts. Exclusions in English.

Preventive, labor, and the components that wear: compressors, motors, boards, valves, within a written list. Exclusions are specific: abuse, acts of water from the floor above, obsolete machines we already told you to replace, work you directed against our recommendation. This is not insurance theater. It is a maintenance relationship with a parts budget we actually hold. It costs what it costs because we have to believe the asset list.

What sits in every agreement

  • Named account engineer and returning technicians
  • Asset list we walked, not a spreadsheet we inherited
  • After-hours coverage and a Manhattan emergency target
  • Parts stocking for the machines that actually fail
  • Quarterly review with engineering — capital, not charm
  • BAS trending if we hold the sequence; a read-only look if we do not

What we will not do

We will not hide exclusions in an appendix. We will not call a filter contract “full coverage.” We will not take a plant we have not surveyed. Residential agreements live with the residences practice — dedicated techs who know the apartment, not a commercial route that stops at a penthouse. Refrigeration and kitchen have their own maintenance page; a restaurant group can hold both under one account team.

Request a proposal with the building address and the asset list if you have one. A walk is the next step.

Questions

Which tier do most buildings take?

Full labor, in commercial. Preventive when the in-house bench is real. Full coverage when the owner wants August to be boring.

Can we mix tiers by asset?

Yes. A critical chiller on full coverage and rooftop units on preventive is a common, honest split.

Do you maintain plants you did not install?

Yes, after a condition survey. We will not inherit fiction.
