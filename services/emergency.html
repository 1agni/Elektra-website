Emergency response

When the plant has already failed. A desk that answers, a truck that moves, a ninety-minute target for Manhattan.

What it is

Emergency is not a marketing hour. The desk is staffed. The on-call technician is named, not a vendor list. We target ninety minutes to Manhattan, longer to the farther edges of Queens and Staten Island, with the number written so you can hold us to it. Chillers down. No-cool. No-heat. Flooded mechanical rooms. A BAS that has gone mute on a Sunday.

We carry common refrigerant, belts, contactors, a loaner VFD language, and the phone numbers for rental tons. We do not pretend every failure is a part we have in the van. We do pretend nothing: if we need a crane at dawn, we say so at midnight.

When a building needs it

When occupied floors are leaving the comfort band. When a hospital is on the last machine. When a data hall is climbing. When a hotel is full and the kitchen hood is down. If you can wait until Tuesday, it is not this page — call maintenance.

How we work

You call (212) 555-0197. You will be asked for address, access, equipment tag if you have it, and the symptom. A technician is dispatched. Engineering is looped if the failure is a plant, not a belt. We stabilize first: temporary cooling, isolation, a bypass that is safe. Then we repair, or we tell you the repair is a project.

Clients on maintenance contracts are first in the queue. That is the honest sentence. We still take buildings we do not maintain when we have the people — we will say if we do not.

What you get

  • A live desk, 24/7
  • Manhattan response target: 90 minutes
  • Stabilization, then repair or a scoped project
  • Rental tons and pumps when the machine is done
  • A written closeout: what failed, what we did, what remains

New York

After-hours access, freight, certificates of insurance, buildings that will not let a van idle in front. We know. FDNY if fuel or life safety is involved. DOB if the repair becomes a replacement. The emergency page of this site is the short version of the same promise.

Questions

Do I need a contract?

No, but contracted sites are dispatched first. That is how a finite bench works.

Ninety minutes always?

A target for Manhattan, not a guarantee against a tunnel. We will tell you an ETA when we dispatch.

Will you condemn a machine at 2am?

If it is unsafe or finished, yes. We would rather say it than run it into a flood.
