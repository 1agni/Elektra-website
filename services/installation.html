Installation

New construction and major replacement. The plant as built, coordinated, lifted, connected, and started — in a city that does not pause for your crane.

What it is

Installation is the part the city sees from a sidewalk: a crane, a cooling tower in the air, a street closure at 4am. It is also the part it does not see: hangers that match the drawing, a weld that will not be a leak in year seven, a BAS cabinet labeled as specified. We self-perform the mechanical work that is ours. We coordinate what is not.

New construction means we are in the BIM, in the weekly with the GC, in the hoist line. Major replacement means we are in an occupied building, which is a different sport. Temporary cooling, phasing, infection control, guest comfort, trading-floor hours — the installation plan is a building plan, not a labor plan.

When a building needs it

When the equipment on the roof is the original and the roof is not. When a hospital cannot take a summer on one machine. When a data hall is going from 8 kW to 30. When the GC needs a mechanical partner who already knows how a Manhattan loading dock works at 5am.

How we work

A superintendent who has done this borough. A coordination model that is actually used. Shop drawings that come back in time for steel. We will not set a machine on a dunnage we have not confirmed. Crane days are scheduled with the city, the neighbor, and the wind. After-hours is normal. Union labor is normal. COIs go out before anyone asks.

We cut, cap, and make safe. We pressure-test in front of the owner’s CxA if they have one, in front of ours if they do not. Start-up is not a vendor waving a laptop; it is a procedure with a name on it. We train the building staff before we leave the roof.

What you get

  • Coordinated installation, BIM or conventional
  • Permits, street and crane logistics, FDNY notifications
  • Temporary services where the building must stay alive
  • Start-up, balancing support, and as-builts that match the plant
  • O&M manuals the chief engineer can find a valve in
  • A warranty conversation that is not a call center

New York

Sidewalk sheds, DOT, DOB, FDNY. Loading docks that only take a 20-foot truck. Roofs with no lay-down. Neighbors with lawyers. We plan for all of it. Shutdown windows are written into the contract because they are the job.

Questions

Do you work as a sub to a GC?

Often. We also work direct to owner on replacement plants. The contract should match the risk.

Union?

Union-aware NYC commercial work. We staff the job as the building and the site require.

Can you install a design you did not draw?

Yes, after a constructability review. We will say so if the set cannot be built as drawn.
