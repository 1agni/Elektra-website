Repair

The machine that is still supposed to run. Occupied floors, a chief engineer on the radio, a part that either exists in LIC or does not.

What it is

Repair is not emergency and it is not a capital project. It is the compressor that is noisy, the VFD that faults on humid mornings, the valve that hunts, the cooling tower basin that is due. We diagnose before we order. We stabilize before we lecture. If the machine is finished, we say so in the first visit rather than bill a season of optimism.

Account buildings get the technician who already knows the tag. New buildings get a walk that produces a written condition, not a shrug. We work on the brands a New York plant actually has — Carrier, Daikin, Trane, York, AAON, Multistack, and the orphaned nameplates that outlived their vendor.

When a building needs it

When the night engineer has been resetting the same alarm. When a floor is warm and the tenant has a clause. When a hospital cannot wait for a capital cycle. When someone else “looked at it” and left a business card.

How we work

Access, COI, freight, the lockout that is real. Measurement: gauges, amp draw, a trend if the BAS still speaks. A verbal to the chief before we leave, a written closeout the same day. If a replacement is the honest move, engineering is in the next conversation — not a surprise invoice.

What you get

  • Diagnosis with evidence, not a guess in the hallway
  • Parts from LIC stock or a named lead time
  • Repair that does not vandalize the adjacent machine
  • A note that the operator can file
  • A capital recommendation only when it is true

New York

After-hours is often the only honest window. Union buildings stay union buildings. We do not open a live plant with a stranger and a flashlight. If the repair is in a residence or a walk-in, that is a different practice page — same desk, different manners.

Repair sits inside an agreement, or it is time-and-material.
