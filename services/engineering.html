Engineering & design

Before a machine is purchased, the building is calculated. We write the basis of design, stamp the set, and model the energy so the filing is not a surprise.

What it is

Engineering at Elektra is a document the field can trust. Heating and cooling loads by space, not by a rule of thumb from a catalog. A basis of design that names outdoor air, filtration, acoustics, redundancy, and the carbon limit the asset actually has. Equipment schedules that fit through the door that exists. Sequences of operation written in English before they are written in the controller.

We produce PE-stamped drawings when the work requires them — and in New York, it usually does. Energy models for Local Law 97 pathways, for utility incentives, and for owners who want to know the plant before they buy it. We coordinate with the architect and the structural engineer because a cooling tower is a load, a sound source, and a silhouette.

When a building needs it

New construction. A gut of a mechanical floor. A law that just became a number on a spreadsheet. A hospital adding ORs. A tower whose 1998 plant is still running on luck. If the last drawing set in the basement is unreadable, you need engineering, not a replacement quote.

We will also tell you when you do not need us to redesign. A like-for-like condenser on a roof that is already correct is a permit and a crane, not a manifesto.

How we work

We start in the building. Meters, nameplates, a walk with the chief engineer, a week of trends if the BAS will give them. Then the studio: loads in the tools we actually use, not a screenshot. Options, priced, with the carbon and acoustic consequences named. The owner picks. We draw.

Coordination is BIM when the project is BIM, 2D when the project is a rooftop in LIC and everyone is tired of the theater. We file. We answer DOB. We sit in the FDNY meeting if smoke or fuel is in the scope. We do not disappear when the contractor is awarded — we remain as design-assist or as engineer of record through commissioning.

What you get

  • Basis of design (BOD) and load report
  • PE-stamped drawings and specifications
  • Energy model and LL97 pathway narrative where relevant
  • Sequences of operation and points list
  • Permit set and response to examiner comments
  • Commissioning script and punch methodology

New York

DOB now, not later. Progress inspections. Special inspections. Energy code that is not a cover sheet. Local Law 97 numbers that match the occupancy you actually have. If the building is landmarked, LPC is a constraint in the BOD, not a surprise in month six. Master plumber and Master HVAC on staff for the work that needs them.

Questions

Do you stamp drawings?

Yes. PE-stamped mechanical drawings for work that requires them. We will not stamp a set we did not control.

Can you work under another engineer of record?

As design-assist, yes. The boundaries have to be written down.

Do you model for LL97?

We model when the pathway depends on the plant. We do not sell a model the owner does not need.
