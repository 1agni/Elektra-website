Residences · 04

Brownstone is not a penthouse.

VRF/VRV, hydronic, dedicated outdoor air, rooms with their own climate. The envelope decides. The brochure does not.

VRF / VRV

The usual language of a high-floor apartment with limited shaft and a terrace that can take condensers. Heat recovery when simultaneous heat and cool is real — a gym next to a glass corner. Branch boxes where the closet allows. Refrigerant management as a safety conversation, not a footnote. We like the brands we already service in commercial: Daikin, Mitsubishi, LG, Samsung, and the rest of the daily list. We will not force VRF into a house that wants water.

Hydronic

Townhouses and brownstones often want radiant, fan coils, or a mixed system that can use a boiler that is already there — or a heat pump that is arriving. Quiet, zonal, friendly to tall windows. The plant lives in a cellar that floods in a storm; we design as if that is true. Dedicated outdoor air sits on top, not as an afterthought in a bath fan.

Dedicated outdoor air

Tight renovations starve rooms of air unless someone draws a path. ERV/HRV sized to occupancy and to cooking, not to a sticker. Makeup for a kitchen hood that actually extracts. We coordinate with the kitchen practice when the hood is commercial-grade in a home — it happens.

Specialty rooms

Wine storage is a climate, not a decorative fridge: temperature, humidity, vibration, a condenser that does not announce itself in the dining room. Gyms are latent load and acoustics. Staff rooms and laundry are real occupancies. We put them on the load sheet.

Brownstone versus penthouse

A brownstone is envelope, cellar, chimney, neighbors, LPC. A penthouse is structure, terrace, wind, the building’s risers, the board. Both are occupied below or beside. Both fail when someone copies a suburban drawing. We start with the house that exists.
