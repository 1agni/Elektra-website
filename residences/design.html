Residences · 03

The air, drawn with the millwork.

Gut renovation HVAC. Load, acoustics, outdoor air, the closet that architecture already spent. We sit with the architect and the GC, not after them.

What it is

A renovation is a chance to put the climate in the architecture instead of on it. We calculate loads from the envelope you are actually building, not from a rule of thumb that assumes a 1990s condo. We size outdoor air as a dedicated path when the apartment is tight. We write acoustics as NC targets for bedrooms and for the mechanical closet that shares a wall with one. We coordinate grilles, returns, and the depth of a cabinet with the millworker — in the drawing, not in a punch-list argument.

Who is in the room

Architect. GC. Sometimes the board’s engineer. Sometimes LPC if the roof or a window is involved. We produce a basis of design a household can read, a PE set when the DOB requires it, and a sequence the technician can commission. We file. We do not ask the architect to invent a louver the landmark staff will refuse.

Constraints we treat as design

Floor-to-floor. A terrace that is also a neighbor’s view. Structural capacity for condensers. Electrical service that may not accept another 60 amps without a building conversation. Steam that is leaving, or is not. Quiet hours during construction because the floors below are occupied — they almost always are.

What you get

  • Loads, BOD, drawings, and a sequence
  • Millwork and grille coordination
  • Filings and a commissioning script
  • A handover the house manager can keep
