Emergency · 24/7

(212) 555-0197

The desk answers. Plant, apartment, or box — say which. If you can wait until Tuesday, call the studio instead: (212) 555-0140.

What counts

No-cool in an occupied commercial building. No-heat in season. A chiller down when you are already on the last machine. Flooded plant. A BAS that has gone mute on a critical floor. A hospital, hotel, or data hall leaving its band. Kitchen exhaust that has stopped in a full house.

Residential no-cool or no-heat in a luxury home we would actually take — a full-floor VRF, a townhouse hydronic, a wine room that is leaving band over inventory that cannot wait. Floor protection still happens; speed does not license a scratched parquet.

Refrigeration product-loss: a walk-in or rack leaving temperature with food inside, a leak you can smell, ice dead for a full house, a DOH morning. Tell the desk it is a box. We treat it as a clock.

If it is a filter and a Monday, it is maintenance.

The first hour

Dispatch with an ETA. Manhattan target: ninety minutes. We tell you if we are longer. Stabilize: isolate, temporary tons, a bypass that is safe, door discipline on a box, protection on a floor. Engineering looped if it is a plant. A verbal to the chief, the house manager, or the chef, then a written closeout. If the machine is finished, we will say so at night rather than run it into a worse morning.

Contracted maintenance sites are first in the queue — commercial, residential, and refrigeration agreements alike. We still take others when the bench allows — we will say if it does not.
